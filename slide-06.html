Lead With the Issue

The most common mistake is burying the point under cushioning until no one can find it.

Buried

"So, things have mostly been going well, and I really appreciate the team, and there's just one small thing I wanted to maybe touch on if that's okay…"

The listener braces and guesses
Sounds evasive
Clear

"I need to flag a timeline risk on the integration. I want to walk you through it and agree on a path in the next ten minutes."

The listener knows what this is about
Sounds in control
A clear opening names the issue, the stakes, and your intent in the first three sentences
Issue Stakes Intent

Directness is not harshness. It is respect for the other person's time and attention.
